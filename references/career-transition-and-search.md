# Career transition and job-search execution

**Sources:** Herminia Ibarra, *Working Identity: Unconventional Strategies for Reinventing Your Career* (2003), especially chs. 2–8 and the methodological appendix; Steve Dalton, *The 2-Hour Job Search: Using Technology to Get the Right Job Faster* (supplied revised text, copyright 2012/2020), Parts 1–3. Links to other modules connect these candidate-side methods to work requirements, organizational context, and hiring evidence.
**Trigger:** changing fields or professional identity; comparing direct entry and bridge roles; building an employer list, networking plan, informational conversations, application sequence, or follow-up workflow.
**What this file is:** two connected procedures: learn what career direction to pursue, then organize a search around provisional role hypotheses. The integrated experiment and weekly plan are advisor synthesis, not promised outcomes from either author.

> **Vintage note.** Ibarra's mechanisms and Dalton's prioritization/learning logic are durable guides, not guarantees. Platform features, tools, email-discovery methods, response-rate claims, application systems, recruiting calendars, visa rules and employer policies are perishable. Check current official application instructions and deadlines. “Two-hour” refers to the setup method, not a promise of a job within two hours.

## 1. Decide whether the uncertainty is about direction or execution

If a candidate understands the target work and has relevant evidence but lacks access or organization, emphasize Dalton's workflow. If the work, identity, preferences or transition path remain uncertain, emphasize Ibarra's **test-and-learn** process. Most transitions require both. More outreach cannot resolve a badly specified role target by itself, and reflection without contact with the work cannot test a career hypothesis.

Before planning, establish the candidate's current work, target possibilities, time and financial runway, nonnegotiable constraints, and feasible activities. An internationally educated candidate's authorization conditions may limit paid, unpaid, freelance or volunteer activities; do not assume an experiment is permissible because it is small or unpaid. Resolve current legal questions separately before recommending participation. A private exercise using public material can often investigate skills without claiming real employment, but is not legal advice about status.

## 2. Ibarra: working identity changes through practice

**Possible selves** are plausible future identities to try, not one hidden authentic career waiting to be discovered. The **plan-and-implement** model presumes sufficient self-knowledge and a defined end state; **test-and-learn** allows experience to change the goals, assumptions and candidate's understanding of self.

**Provisional selves** are trial ways of doing and presenting work. Keep multiple possibilities alive long enough to observe them. A coherent retrospective story can hide uncertainty and iteration; do not use a successful person's polished biography as evidence that their route was predictable.

The three practices are:

- **Crafting experiments:** try activities with a real connection to the possible work. Projects, bounded assignments, shadowing where permitted, courses involving practice, and limited commitments can expose preferences and capability. Choose experiences that can surprise the candidate, not merely confirm an attractive title.
- **Shifting connections:** meet people and groups outside the network that reinforces the old identity. New practitioners, role models and peers can provide information and recognition the old circle cannot. Existing supporters may sincerely favor continuity. This is a reason to diversify learning relationships, not to abandon supportive people or demand transactional favors.
- **Making sense:** revise explanations of what the candidate has done and is becoming through conversation and experience. A narrative connects true continuity with tested change; it is not a license to retrofit invented certainty or competence.

**Lingering between identities** can be part of learning, not proof of failure. **Small wins** accumulate evidence and confidence before deep change is consolidated. However, Ibarra's account does not license indefinite indecision or disregard for financial, family, health, or legal constraints. Set a review point and a feasible risk boundary.

The evidence is grounded in observed career-transition accounts, not a controlled guarantee for every population. Student transitions, constrained job loss, and international moves may offer less freedom to experiment than some cases in the book. Adapt the scale and sequence while preserving learning through action.

## 3. Make experiments diagnostic

Use this experiment card (**advisor synthesis**):

- **Hypothesis:** a specific task or environment might suit the candidate and be accessible with a defined evidence gap closed.
- **Activity and artifact:** a bounded task resembling the work, with an observable output and truthful attribution of the candidate's contribution.
- **Audience:** someone with relevant experience who can identify mistakes and missing expectations; separate a friendly reaction from substantive evaluation.
- **Constraints:** time, money, confidentiality, permission, authorization and opportunity cost.
- **Learning:** task competence, enjoyment while doing the routine work, response to constraints, and what further preparation is needed. These can diverge.
- **Decision:** continue, revise, deepen through a bridge, or stop; specify evidence that would favor each option and when to review.

A bridge role should accumulate missing task evidence, observers, and access to the target work. Name the link it builds and an exit criterion. A new degree is an experiment only if its costs and commitments are proportionate; investigate lower-cost ways to learn the same thing first. Use [Roles and organizations](roles-and-organizations.md) to identify the actual gap.

For a law-to-product-policy transition, compare a research brief with an operational rule-and-escalation exercise. Practitioner feedback may show strong analysis but weak implementation detail. That finding can justify a focused project or operational bridge rather than another broad credential. Label this example as synthesis, not a claim about available roles or future hiring success.

## 4. Dalton: Prioritize with the LAMP List

**LAMP = List, Advocacy, Motivation, Posting.** It is an employer-prioritization device; it is not a test of person–job fit.

1. **List:** Dalton proposes at least 40 employers, found through dream employers, alumni/affinity employers, actively hiring employers and trending employers. The purpose is to escape a tiny set of famous names. Record a smaller feasible list as an adaptation, not as faithful execution of the complete method. Geography or a genuinely narrow occupation can constrain breadth.
2. **Advocacy:** mark whether a potentially useful affinity/contact route exists. This is a proxy for access, not proof someone will help or a requirement that the candidate already knows the person.
3. **Motivation:** record the candidate's interest consistently. Curiosity is a starting point; revise after evidence rather than let prestige alone determine order.
4. **Posting:** record evidence of relevant hiring activity. Posting is a priority signal, not a guarantee of an opening, eligibility, or invitation. Preserve job-specific deadlines separately.
5. **Sort:** Dalton orders by **Motivation, then Posting, then Advocacy**. Review the top group for obvious mismatches and update genuine interest as information arrives. Do not endlessly perfect the list before acting.

The book's scale and timeboxes are workflow defaults, not validated universal optima. Add role, level, location, verified hard constraints, source/date, deadline and next action as **advisor adaptations**. Check these before spending large effort, but do not erase an employer solely because a fact is unknown. Employer sponsorship history is neither current willingness nor the candidate's legal eligibility.

## 5. Contact: naturalize contacts and bound outreach

**Naturalize** means create a useful point of connection rather than assume all assistance must come from existing friends. Start with people close enough to the target work to offer relevant insight; affinity can make contact easier but does not substitute for expertise. Work a small active employer group, keeping enough alternatives to avoid dependence on one response.

Dalton's **Boosters, Obligates, and Curmudgeons** distinguish willing helpers, obligation-driven contacts, and nonresponders. These are the author's search heuristics, not diagnoses of people. Response speed is noisy: workload, leave, communication norms or a missed message can explain silence. Allocate effort based on observed usefulness without moralizing about the person.

The **6-Point Email** has six constraints:

1. Keep it under 75 words.
2. Request insight or advice rather than job leads.
3. Put the genuine connection first.
4. Formulate the request as a question.
5. Express both a specific and a broader area of interest.
6. Devote more than half the message to the recipient rather than the sender.

This is an informational request, not a deceptive sales pretext. Do not fabricate affinity or pretend to seek advice while hiding an unrelated purpose. Adapt wording to the relationship and actual interest. Drafting is distinct from permission to send; send only when the user authorizes it.

**Illustrative draft, not a source quotation:** “Hello Maya, we both studied at Northbridge. Your move from compliance into policy operations caught my attention. Would you be open to a brief conversation about how your team turns policy into daily decisions, and what you have learned about policy careers more broadly? Your experience at that interface would help me understand the work.” Use only a true shared connection and verified description of the contact.

**3B7 Routine:** for each initial outreach, schedule checks at three and seven **business days**. At 3B without a response, the book recommends approaching another person at that employer, not following up with the first person yet. At 7B, follow up with the original contact. Across employers, searches can proceed in parallel; within one employer, avoid a burst to many contacts. Pause redundant outreach when someone engages and respond promptly.

Treat these intervals as Dalton's defaults. Respect requested timing, holidays, local norms and explicit refusals. A polite bounded follow-up is different from pursuing someone indefinitely. Tracking should contain employer, role hypothesis, contact, true connection, dates, status, promised actions, application deadline, and next action. Use ordinary authorized channels; the book's old tools or contact-finding tactics need current checking.

## 6. Convince through informational learning

Prepare enough to ask informed questions, not to recite the company's website. Dalton separates small talk, questions/answers, and next steps. His **TIARA** order is **Trends, Insights, Advice, Resources, Assignments**:

| Prompt family | What to learn | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| Trends | What is changing in the work | “Which changes most affect the decisions your team makes?” |
| Insights | What experienced practitioners notice | “What do outsiders misunderstand about this role?” |
| Advice | How a person might prepare or choose | “Given these two kinds of experience, what gap should I investigate first?” |
| Resources | Where to continue learning | “Which materials or communities help someone understand the actual work?” |
| Assignments | A concrete way to learn by doing | “What small exercise would reveal whether I understand the problem?” |

These questions should elicit useful information and allow a relationship to develop. Do not treat TIARA as a device that obligates a referral. If a conversation becomes evaluative, answer honestly, distinguish what is known from what would need investigation, and avoid pretending an informal meeting cannot affect hiring.

Close by confirming a useful next step. Send a specific thank-you, complete agreed actions, and report what was learned. Dalton recommends recurring follow-up with useful learning updates; adapt cadence to consent and the relationship. A genuine update is preferable to repeated requests for help without acting on advice. Keep source, team and date attached to employer information.

## 7. Application sequencing and iteration

Dalton favors informational contact before applying, with an exception for imminent deadlines. Preserve the mechanism—learn what evidence matters and seek informed advocacy—without treating his timing as universal policy. Current employer instructions, rolling review, early closure, or formal campus/public-sector processes may favor immediate application while networking proceeds separately. Never miss a known deadline to obey a networking rule or assume a referral eliminates a required application.

Review the funnel by stage: employers investigated → relevant contacts → substantive conversations → task evidence improved → applications → assessments → offers. Low response may call for better contacts or clearer requests; conversations without appropriate opportunities may call for a different market; interview difficulty may call for targeted preparation. These are hypotheses, not causal diagnoses from tiny samples.

Keep an execution review separate from a direction review. A disciplined search can be pursuing the wrong work; a promising identity experiment can still need a better access route. At a scheduled review, decide what to stop, maintain or change using the observed evidence. Do not mistake activity volume for learning or treat every rejection as evidence against the entire transition.

## Decision rules

- When direction is uncertain, test possible selves through work and new relationships before demanding a final identity.
- When direction is sufficiently defined, turn it into ranked employers, specific contacts and dated next actions.
- Keep LAMP, the 6-Point Email, 3B7 and TIARA precise; mark changes as adaptations.
- Make narratives and applications truthful records of tested capability, not aspirational biographies.
- Let current deadlines and employer instructions govern application timing.
- End with one finishable next action, the evidence it will produce and the condition for revising the plan.
